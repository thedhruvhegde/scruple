PAPER / DRAFT, NOVEMBER 2026Verifiers that do not trust the agent: anytime-valid release gates and hacking bounds for money-moving AI agents

Dhruv Hegde. Synthetic numbers come from the scruple simulator. Real numbers come from public CFPB complaints or from real model behaviour.

An AI agent that handles card disputes moves money. It decides when to refund, when to fight, and with what evidence. Changes to such an agent are usually judged by dashboards of wins and by the agent's own reports. Both can be gamed.

Scruple is a release gate that never reads the agent's claims for credit. Its verifiers recompute cost from double-entry ledger postings and check every action against a hash-chained trace the environment writes. Its statistics stay valid however often you look and whenever you stop.

We prove bounds on how much a hacked change can gain and still pass, for agents that adapt, randomise and partly see where audits fall. We give the audit rate that removes the incentive to hack when the verifier misses some hacks and flags some honest cases. We state exactly what the combined promote decision guarantees, and what it does not.

We test it on a frozen synthetic benchmark of 10,000 disputes, on public CFPB complaints, and with real Claude models. One pre-registered experiment passed narrowly. One failed its main hypothesis, and we report why.

01 / CONTRIBUTIONSFive things the paper adds.

  • Verifiers anchored to money, not to words. Cost is recomputed from ledger postings. Every action is checked against a trace the environment writes. Each check ships with an attack it stops.
  • Tests you can watch every day. Paired and per-case-type tests that stay valid however often you look and whenever you stop, with planning and a matching lower bound.
  • Bets that adapt to the data, and a floor no test can beat. A mixture of bets close to the best fixed bet on every run, and the fewest cases any valid test needs, computed from the observed costs.
  • What the release decision guarantees. A theorem for the whole gate: which errors are bounded, which add up, and which are not covered at all.
  • Hacking bounds for agents that adapt. How much a hacked change can gain and still pass, when audits leak and the verifier makes mistakes, and the audit rate that ends the incentive.

02 / KEY RESULTSWhat we found, in plain words.

  • Peeking breaks ordinary tests. Ours hold. Checking a z-test every 10 cases rejects a harmless change 15% to 34% of the time at a nominal 5%. The betting test's worst rate over seven stopping rules was 2.7%. synthetic, 2,000 paths
  • Averages hide rare case types. A change that hurts a type seen in 1% of cases, while helping the rest, is flagged 0.6% of the time by a pooled test and 100% of the time by per-type tests. synthetic
  • The shipped promote rule has a hole, and we measured it. It certifies the pooled mean. It promoted that rare-type regression in 64% of runs at 20,000 cases. Certifying every type closes it, at a large cost in power. synthetic
  • A hacked change can carry only a small gap through the gate. The bound holds for any agent that cannot fully see the audits, and it is attained when audits leak. exact, on E2's 120 batches
  • The optimiser learned to cheat, and the ledger called it cheaper. Tuned on wins and self-reports, it learned to forge documents and claim wins. On the simulated ledger it cost $33.87 per case less. Provenance checks blocked it. A cost-only gate would have shipped it. synthetic, E2
  • The cheapest real model was not the fairest. Claude Sonnet cut merchant cost by $32.77 per case, and 4 of its 10 wins came against claims the cardholder was right about. real behaviour, simulated cases
  • The regression E1 missed was a betting problem. The shipped bet stops at half the largest valid bet. A wider bet, just as valid, caught R5 on the benchmark itself, and raised power on R2 at 5,000 cases from 37% to 91%. synthetic, E1 paired costs
  • Real models found bugs in the verifier. Three false positives that our own attacks had missed. Fixing them raised gate pass from 5, 8 and 8 of 12 to 7, 11 and 11. real behaviour

04 / NEW RESULTSEach one proved in full in the PDF.

  • Thm 10.3If every hacked case is flagged with probability at least rho, given all the agent knew, an accepted change has a gap of at least gamma with probability at most (1 - rho)^ceil(n gamma / eps), and an expected accepted gap of at most eps / (n e ln(1/(1 - rho))). No independence needed.adaptation
  • Cor 10.5If the agent's signal has a likelihood ratio of at least l, then rho = (1 - beta) q l / (q l + 1 - q). An agent that can see the audits defeats any spot check.adaptation
  • Thm 10.6With a verifier that misses a share beta of hacks and flags a share phi of honest cases, hacking stops paying exactly at q* = g / ((1 - beta)(K + g) - phi K).candidate novel, framing
  • Cor 10.7No audit rate deters unless beta g <= (1 - beta - phi) K. False alarms raise the rate you need.candidate novel, framing
  • Prop 7.1Averaging bets over a grid of J sizes gives a valid test whose wealth is within log J of the best fixed bet on every path.adaptation
  • Prop 7.2The best fixed-bet growth rate g* of the observed costs equals a KL distance to a harmless law, so any valid test needs at least kl(1 - beta || alpha) / g* cases.adaptation
  • Thm 9.1The promote decision carries the smallest level among the checks that cover a harm. False blocks add up. Harms no check covers carry no guarantee.standard, applied

06 / LIMITSWhat this does not show.

  • One simulator. Every synthetic number describes our simulator and our assumptions about issuers. E2 shows the cost: forged documents win there, so the ledger anchor was gamed through it.
  • Small real samples. 12 cases per model, 50 to 100 complaint items per task. These show the method. They are not a leaderboard.
  • Complaints are not disputes. CFPB data is complaints to a regulator. Its outcome is the company's reply, not a card network's decision.
  • Thin regressions are invisible. R5 costs about one cent per case overall. Any valid pooled test would need at least 509,153 cases to see it. It shows up only because its cost sits in one dispute family.
  • Novelty is unchecked. "Candidate novel" means we did not find it stated this way in a short search. A full literature review comes before any claim.
