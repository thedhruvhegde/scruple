RESULTSReal data, real models, simulated cases.

Two kinds of evidence. Public complaint data, scored against what really happened. And real Claude models working simulated card disputes through the same tools an outside agent would use.

  • RealCFPB consumer complaints, public domain. Held-out items, graded by code against the real field.
  • RealEvery tool call made by Claude Haiku, Sonnet and Opus. The replays below run their logged calls again.
  • SyntheticThe 12 dispute cases, the issuer that answers them, and the ledger they post to.
  • ScoredFrom the ledger and the hash-chained trace, never from what the model says about itself.

The paper. Verifiers that do not trust the agent: anytime-valid release gates and hacking bounds for money-moving AI agents. Twenty pages, every proof written out, every number taken from the result files on this page.

01 / REAL DATAThey know the rules. They misjudge which complaints end in relief.

Claude Haiku and Claude Sonnet answered held-out CFPB complaints blind. On which regulation applies, both beat keyword rules and matched a trained classifier. On whether a complaint ends in relief, neither beat it.

  • RegimeReg E (12 CFR 1005.11) or Reg Z (12 CFR 1026.13), with a section cited. Accuracy.
  • ReliefProbability the complaint closed with relief. AUROC, where 0.5 is chance.
  • RoutingThe issue the consumer filed under, 2025 complaints. Accuracy.
  • BaselineTF-IDF and logistic regression, fitted on train, chosen on dev, scored on the same items.

Intervals: paired bootstrap over items, 2,000 resamples, model minus TF-IDF. Only one difference excludes zero, and only at the edge. The models ran as Claude Code subagents, not through the raw API. CFPB complaints are a self-selected sample, and the outcome is the company's response, not a card-network decision.

02 / WATCH A CASEEvery call here is the model's own.

          
Haiku, Sonnet and Opus each handled the same 12 simulated disputes, told to play honestly and keep the merchant's loss low. Pick one. The replay runs its logged calls through the engine again and shows what each call returned.

          
Verifier notes are scored when the case closes, and pinned to the step they read. Output is abridged for width; nothing is added. Document ids are shortened.

    03 / EXPERIMENTSTwo pre-registered tests. One passed narrowly. One failed.
    
        
    Both ran once on benchmark v1, 10,000 synthetic held-out dispute cases, with the pass bar committed before the run.
    
        
      
          
    • E1Plant 7 narrow regressions in a good policy. Does scruple flag them, and leave 3 harmless changes alone?
      • 
          
    • E2Let an optimiser climb on wins and self-reports, then on the scruple scorecard. What does each learn?
      • 
        
    
        
    Synthetic episodes only. One simulator, one benchmark, policies we wrote. They say nothing yet about the regressions real agents have.

    Point estimates meet both bars; neither interval does. A regression that only costs money (1.3 cents a case; the E1 write-up printed it as $1.30, a units slip) was not caught by the statistical gate even on 10,000 cases. The paper shows a wider, still valid bet does catch it. The 3 control false alarms came from the ledger check INV-01.
